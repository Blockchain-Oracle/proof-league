# Digest: network-blockspace

**Summary:** Structurally the weakest family, for a reason confirmed from primaries: the EVM exposes BASEFEE (EIP-3198) and BLOBBASEFEE (EIP-7516) but there is NO opcode for block gas used — so ETH burned per day, block fullness and L1 revenue are not computable on-chain at all, by any keeper. That kills the most legible member outright. What remains is reachable only via SNAPSHOT-KEEPER, and 2026 hurts it twice: (1) base fee sits at ~0.15-0.5 gwei against a 60M gas limit, so a 10x spike over ~20 blocks costs only ~2.3 ETH (~$7k) — spot-basefee markets are buyable; (2) EIP-7918 now floors the blob base fee at a fixed ~1/16 ratio to the execution base fee, and post-Fusaka capacity (14 target/21 max, 20-30% utilization) means the blob fee mostly hugs that floor — it is a duplicate of the base fee with worse legibility, not an independent source. The only survivor at demo grade is the base fee DAILY AVERAGE via a Uniswap-v3-style accumulator whose final poke emits the mean: sustaining an elevated base fee for 24h costs thousands of ETH, so TWAP is the whole defence. Its narrative asset is unusually strong — Vitalik asked for exactly this on Dec 8, 2025 ("a prediction market on the basefee"), and the published objection (Hasu: "no natural short side") is a talking point, not a blocker. PART 3 VERDICT: the snapshot-keeper pattern is cheap and proven. Prior art is Sky/MakerDAO's permissionless OSM poke() (guarded by block.timestamp/hop), Uniswap v3 observe() for the accumulator, and Hedgehog Protocol's on-chain logarithmic moving average of gas prices. A poke reading state and emitting one event is ~30-60k gas = ~$0.07 at 2026 mainnet fees, free on Sepolia. Gas is a non-issue; TRUST and LIVENESS are the real costs — the contract must do pure on-chain reads only (the moment a value arrives as a poke argument the keeper is an oracle), must pin the read to block.timestamp >= deadline and record the actual read block (otherwise the poker chooses when to read and therefore chooses the answer), and a missed poke is a stuck market, not a NO. One unresolved high-value question: the Chainlink Fast Gas/Gwei feed is the ONLY candidate here that settles natively from one tx with no keeper of ours, but etherscan.io 403'd every automated fetch and the Blockscout mirror returned only a page title, so I have zero observed recent activity — a human must open 0x169E633A2D1E6c10dD91238Ba11c4A708dfEF37C in a browser before anything is planned around it, especially since Chainlink's own policy schedules low-usage feeds for deprecation.

## Candidates
### 1. Base fee daily average (accumulator + final poke) — the Vitalik gas-futures product — fit 4/5
- **question_example**: Will Ethereum's average gas price on Friday be above or below 0.4 gwei?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Purpose-built snapshot contract (must be deployed; none found on mainnet). Poke every N blocks accumulating block.basefee * elapsed into storage, Uniswap-v3 tick-cumulative style; the final poke at/after the deadline computes (cum_end - cum_start)/(t_end - t_start) and emits SettlementSnapshot(uint256 marketId, uint256 avgBasefeeWei, uint64 samples). Answer carried in the avgBasefeeWei field; marketId indexed as topic1.
- **cadence**: daily (any window length; forever, no curation)
- **uncertainty**: Cannot be computed or simulated in advance — a 24h mean base fee depends on aggregate future demand for blockspace from thousands of unrelated actors. No forecast method resolves it.
- **manipulation**: Strong. The cheap attack (fill ~20 consecutive blocks to 10x the base fee) costs ~2.3 ETH / ~$7k derived from 60M gas limit, 0.5 gwei base and the 12.5% per-block cap — but that only holds for minutes. Sustaining an elevated base fee across a 24h window means paying inflated fees on ~7,200 blocks of 60M gas: thousands of ETH. Outcome-makers CAN bet (anyone who transacts moves the base fee), but no individual moves a daily mean. TWAP is the entire defence — do not ship the spot variant.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT — the final keeper poke tx emits the mean in one event. Caveat: the accumulator state is built by many earlier pokes, so a missed keeper run biases the average; emit the sample count so a proof consumer can judge coverage.
- **live_evidence**: Base fee always exists, so the source cannot die. Regime data: standard gas ~0.15 gwei on 2026-05-05, daily averages ~0.5 gwei through April 2026 — https://ethereum.org/latest/building-on-ethereum-in-2026/ (Ethereum Foundation). Demand validation: Vitalik's 2025-12-08 call for a basefee prediction market — https://www.theblock.co/post/381677/vitalik-buterin-pushes-for-trustless-gas-futures-market-to-hedge-ethereum-fees-sparking-debate
- **notes**: Best-in-slice. Loses the 5 only on legibility: 2026 base fees are compressed into 0.15-0.5 gwei, so the question reads '0.4 vs 0.5 gwei' rather than a visceral spread, and it carries a keeper-coverage dependency the purer candidates don't. Prior art for the accumulator: Uniswap v3 observe(); Hedgehog Protocol already mints a Base Fee token priced off an on-chain logarithmic moving average of gas prices (2026 liveness unverified — design reference only).
### 2. Base fee spot snapshot at a scheduled block — fit 3/5
- **question_example**: Will Ethereum gas be above 1 gwei at 8pm Friday?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Same snapshot contract, no accumulator: one poke at/after the deadline emits SettlementSnapshot(marketId, block.basefee) plus the read block number. Answer in the value field.
- **cadence**: daily or hourly
- **uncertainty**: Genuinely uncertain — the base fee at a specific future minute is unknowable in advance.
- **manipulation**: WEAK — this is the disqualifying flaw. At a 60M gas limit and 0.5 gwei base, filling one block costs ~0.03 ETH; the base fee rises at most 12.5% per full block, so ~20 consecutive full blocks gives 10x for roughly 2.3 ETH total (~$7k at $3k ETH). A bettor with $10k owns any single-block outcome. Outcome-makers bet freely and cheaply.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT — one poke tx, one event.
- **live_evidence**: Base fee levels and 60M gas limit both from https://ethereum.org/latest/building-on-ethereum-in-2026/ (Ethereum Foundation, May 2026 data).
- **notes**: Simplest possible thing to demo and the easiest to explain on stage, but do not ship it as the flagship — the manipulation cost is within reach of a hackathon judge's pocket money. Use it as the tutorial market, then show the TWAP variant as the answer.
### 3. Chainlink Fast Gas/Gwei feed AnswerUpdated — fit 3/5
- **question_example**: Will Chainlink's fast gas price print above 1 gwei before Friday close?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Chainlink aggregator behind proxy 0x169E633A2D1E6c10dD91238Ba11c4A708dfEF37C on mainnet. The aggregator emits AnswerUpdated(int256 indexed current, uint256 indexed roundId, uint256 updatedAt); the answer is the `current` topic. No keeper of ours required.
- **cadence**: per deviation threshold / heartbeat (unverified in 2026)
- **uncertainty**: Genuinely uncertain — tracks the same future blockspace demand as candidate 1.
- **manipulation**: Two layers: the underlying gas price is cheaply movable (see candidate 2), and the reported value is DON-mediated, so settlement trusts Chainlink node operators rather than pure on-chain reads. Rule 5 trusted-publisher case — must be disclosed.
- **single_tx_settlement**: YES — native. Each AnswerUpdated emission is a single tx whose receipt carries the answer in topic1. The only candidate in this slice needing no snapshot contract at all.
- **live_evidence**: UNVERIFIED — this is the blocker. etherscan.io returned HTTP 403 to every automated fetch and the Blockscout mirror returned only a page title, so I observed NO recent transaction. Chainlink docs still categorize Fast Gas as a live Technical Feed, but the deprecation policy explicitly schedules low-usage feeds for shutdown: https://docs.chain.link/data-feeds/deprecating-feeds (Chainlink Labs). Address confirmed via search result title on etherscan.io.
- **notes**: Would be a 4 if liveness were confirmed. ACTION: a human must open the address in a browser and confirm a recent AnswerUpdated before anything is planned around this. Gas-price feeds are exactly the low-usage kind Chainlink deprecates.
### 4. Block gas limit crosses a threshold — fit 3/5
- **question_example**: Will Ethereum's block gas limit be above 60,000,000 on October 1?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Snapshot contract poked at/after the deadline reads block.gaslimit and emits SettlementSnapshot(marketId, gasLimit). Answer in the value field.
- **cadence**: irregular — a handful of moves per year
- **uncertainty**: Excellent. The outcome is the aggregate voting behaviour of thousands of validators choosing a client config — pure human coordination, not simulatable.
- **manipulation**: Effectively unbuyable. Moving the gas limit requires a validator majority, so the attack cost is control of the network. Best manipulation resistance in the entire slice.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT — one poke tx reading block.gaslimit.
- **live_evidence**: Fusaka activated 2025-12-03 and EIP-7935 standardized 60M as the new default (up from 30M during 2025); Glamsterdam expected H2 2026 with BALs + ePBS targeting ~200M — https://ethereum.org/latest/building-on-ethereum-in-2026/ (Ethereum Foundation). Live campaign coverage: 2026 gas limit roadmap 60M to 200M — https://www.bitget.com/amp/news/detail/12560605122695
- **notes**: Fails only the CONTINUOUS SUPPLY bar, and fails it hard — this cannot be the daily engine. Treat it as a hero/featured market that showcases perfect uncertainty and perfect manipulation resistance, running alongside a daily source. Very topical in 2026 given the Glamsterdam push.
### 5. block.prevrandao (RANDAO) snapshot — fit 3/5
- **question_example**: Will Ethereum's randomness beacon come up even or odd at midnight?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Snapshot contract poked at/after the deadline emits SettlementSnapshot(marketId, uint256(block.prevrandao)) or its parity. Answer in the value field.
- **cadence**: daily forever, zero curation
- **uncertainty**: Perfect — the beacon RANDAO mix is cryptographically unpredictable before the block is proposed.
- **manipulation**: Essentially none. The deadline block's proposer can withhold to bias the result by at most one bit, at the cost of a missed block reward. No bettor without a validator slot can touch it.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT — one poke tx.
- **live_evidence**: PREVRANDAO has been the post-Merge randomness source continuously; the 2026 network is running Fusaka with no change to it — https://ethereum.org/latest/building-on-ethereum-in-2026/ (Ethereum Foundation).
- **notes**: HONEST DOWNGRADE: this is a lottery, not a prediction market. There is no skill, no information edge, nothing to be right about — it is the cleanest possible demo of the settlement machinery and the worst possible demo of the product thesis. Scoring it 3 rather than 5 despite passing alive/daily/legible/uncertain/single-tx, because a judge will ask 'what is anyone predicting?' and there is no answer.
### 6. EIP-7002 validator exit-queue fee — fit 2/5
- **question_example**: Will the cost to request a validator exit spike above X this week?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Snapshot contract staticcalls the withdrawal-request predeploy 0x00000961Ef480Eb55e80D19ad83579A64c007002 with EMPTY calldata (the contract interprets zero-length input as a fee query and returns get_fee()), then emits SettlementSnapshot(marketId, feeWei).
- **cadence**: continuous / weekly windows
- **uncertainty**: Genuinely uncertain — the fee is exponential in excess withdrawal requests over a target of 2 per block, so it measures validator exit panic. Unforecastable human behaviour.
- **manipulation**: Endogenous: an attacker spams withdrawal requests to inflate the fee. Self-priced (they pay the rising fee, MIN_FEE * e**(excess/UPDATE_FRACTION)) but not prohibitive near the low end where excess is small. Outcome-makers can bet.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT — one poke tx doing the staticcall and emitting.
- **live_evidence**: Predeploy address, empty-calldata fee-read mechanism and exponential fee formula confirmed at https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7002 (Ethereum Foundation, status Final). I did NOT verify current fee levels or request volume on-chain this run.
- **notes**: Genuinely novel and network-level, but fails LEGIBILITY badly — nobody understands 'exit queue fee' in seconds, and explaining it burns the whole demo window. Keep as an exotic in a long tail, not a headline.
### 7. Blob base fee at deadline — fit 2/5
- **question_example**: Will Ethereum's blob fee be above X gwei on Friday?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Snapshot contract poked at/after the deadline reads block.blobbasefee (BLOBBASEFEE opcode, EIP-7516) and emits SettlementSnapshot(marketId, blobBaseFeeWei).
- **cadence**: daily
- **uncertainty**: WEAK IN 2026 — largely disqualifying. EIP-7918 (Final) stops excess_blob_gas from decreasing whenever BLOB_BASE_COST(=2**13) * base_fee_per_gas > GAS_PER_BLOB * base_fee_per_blob_gas, pinning the blob fee to roughly a 1/16 ratio of execution cost. With post-Fusaka capacity at 14 target / 21 max and utilization at 20-30%, the blob fee hugs that floor, making it a near-deterministic function of the execution base fee rather than an independent quantity.
- **manipulation**: Inherits base fee manipulability (since the floor tracks base fee) plus direct blob spam when utilization is high. Cheaper than it looks in the floor regime.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT — one poke tx.
- **live_evidence**: EIP-7918 formula and constants: https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7918 (Ethereum Foundation, Final). Blob target raised to 14 / max 21 by Jan 2026 with utilization falling to 20-30%: https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/01/08/ethereum-bumps-blob-capacity-as-it-gears-for-fusaka-upgrade (CoinDesk). Mid-July 2026 burn muted and blob space plentiful: https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/ethereum-blob-fees-cheaper-l2s-reduce-eth-burn (CryptoDaily).
- **notes**: Answers the brief's Part 2 question directly: NO, blob fees are not usefully volatile in 2026. The 2024-25 story of spikes to 100+ gwei does not describe the current regime. This is a duplicate of the base fee market with worse legibility — drop it.
### 8. Beacon deposit contract balance / deposit count crossing a threshold — fit 2/5
- **question_example**: Will total ETH ever staked cross 40 million by September 30?
- **settlement_mechanism**: Snapshot contract reads the deposit contract's balance (or calls get_deposit_count()) at/after the deadline and emits SettlementSnapshot(marketId, value).
- **cadence**: continuous, but a one-way ratchet
- **uncertainty**: Genuinely uncertain near a threshold — depends on aggregate future staking demand.
- **manipulation**: WEAK. Outcome-makers can bet and the attack is nearly free in real terms: a whale deposits to force the crossing, then exits later and recovers the capital. Manipulation cost is opportunity cost plus exit delay, not burned value.
- **single_tx_settlement**: ONLY-VIA-SNAPSHOT for the cumulative value. (Individual DepositEvent emissions are single-tx but only prove one deposit, never the total.)
- **live_evidence**: Not verified on-chain this run — I did not confirm current deposit contract balance or recent deposit activity. Related 2026 supply figure for context: ETH supply ~121.88M mid-July 2026, https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/ethereum-blob-fees-cheaper-l2s-reduce-eth-burn (CryptoDaily).
- **notes**: Monotonic ratchet makes late-window markets one-sided and boring, and the refundable-manipulation flaw is real. Listed for completeness; not recommended.
### 9. ETH burned per day / block fullness / L1 fee revenue — fit 1/5
- **question_example**: How much ETH will Ethereum burn today?
- **settlement_mechanism**: NONE EXISTS. This is the key structural finding of the slice: the EVM exposes BASEFEE (EIP-3198) and BLOBBASEFEE (EIP-7516) but there is NO opcode for block gas used, and no mainnet contract stores cumulative burn. A snapshot keeper cannot read what the EVM does not expose, so burn is unreachable on-chain — not merely hard.
- **cadence**: n/a
- **uncertainty**: Would be genuinely uncertain, which makes the unreachability especially painful — this is the most legible question in the whole family.
- **manipulation**: n/a
- **single_tx_settlement**: NO — unreachable. Would require a trusted off-chain publisher writing burn to an Ethereum contract; I searched and found none on mainnet.
- **live_evidence**: Burn is real and measurable off-chain (L1 fees ~$227k/day mid-July 2026, https://cryptodaily.co.uk/2026/07/ethereum-blob-fees-cheaper-l2s-reduce-eth-burn, CryptoDaily) but no on-chain publisher was found this run.
- **notes**: Directly answers the brief's Part 2 question 'ETH burned per day (needs one emitting tx — exists?)': NO, and it cannot be fixed with a keeper. Document this as a hard boundary so nobody re-proposes it.
### 10. Gas used by a specific transaction — fit 1/5
- **question_example**: How much gas will this transaction use?
- **settlement_mechanism**: gasUsed is in the receipt, so it is trivially decodable — reachability is not the problem.
- **cadence**: n/a
- **uncertainty**: NONE — fully simulatable in advance via eth_estimateGas or a fork simulation. The product owner's own disqualifying example.
- **manipulation**: n/a — anyone who can simulate knows the answer for free, which is worse than manipulable.
- **single_tx_settlement**: YES technically, which is exactly the trap: it looks perfect on the receipt-decoding axis and fails completely on the uncertainty axis.
- **live_evidence**: n/a — disqualified on quality bar 6, not on liveness.
- **notes**: Included deliberately as a documented dead end. It is the most seductive false positive in this slice because it satisfies every referee rule and no quality bar.
