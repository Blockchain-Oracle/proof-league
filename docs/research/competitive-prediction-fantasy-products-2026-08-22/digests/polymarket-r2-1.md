# Digest: polymarket (round 2)

**Summary:** The biggest find this round is a split in Polymarket's settlement story: the CFTC-designated US venue (QCX LLC) does not use UMA at all — its docs say only that contracts settle at $1.00/$0.00 and that "All settlements are final in accordance with the Polymarket US Exchange Rulebook," with the rulebook defining "Source Agency" as the Exchange itself resolving against a declared Primary/Secondary/Tertiary source hierarchy, and no user-facing dispute or appeal path documented. So the UMA conflict-of-interest liability the WSJ exposed attaches to the offshore book only, and the US venue has already adopted the Kalshi-style self-resolution model the WSJ contrasted Polymarket against. The Polymarket US fee schedule is now primary-sourced (effective 2026-07-01: Fee = Θ × C × p × (1−p), taker Θ=0.06 capped $1.50, maker Θ=−0.0125 rebate, volume rebates to 50%) and contradicts round 1's affiliate claim of 0.30%/0.20%, which was the wrong functional form entirely. The signup→first-bet gap is closed: Google/Apple SSO, name/DOB/address, mostly instant automatic KYC (ID + selfie fallback, 3–5 business days if manual), KYC hard-gates both deposit and trade, and debit/ACH deposits grant instant buying power while clearing — so time-to-first-trade is minutes despite 3–4 day settlement. Withdrawal friction lives on the fiat rails, not in pUSD: funds-in-flight cannot be withdrawn until cleared, withdrawals must return to the original payment method, and processing is FIFO by deposit order for AML. pUSD itself is a plain 1:1 USDC-backed ERC-20 on Polygon with onchain wrap/unwrap, no disclosed yield model, and no documented redemption delay or fee — its only friction vector is admin pause gates. The WSJ piece is now anchored to a specific citable article but its statistics remain second-hand because wsj.com is blocked to the fetch tool.

## Contradictions
- Round 1 carried an affiliate claim of '0.30% taker / 0.20% maker rebate' for Polymarket US. The primary fee schedule at docs.polymarket.us/fees shows a different functional form entirely: Fee = Θ × C × p × (1 − p), taker Θ = 0.06, maker Θ = −0.0125, effective 2026-07-01. The affiliate figure should be discarded.
- Round 1 recorded that 'the help center and docs mention only UMA' and that non-UMA alternatives appeared solely in secondary commentary. That is true of the offshore docs but false of the company overall: the Polymarket US docs and rulebook describe an exchange-resolved, rulebook-governed settlement process with no oracle involvement. The non-UMA mechanism is not exploratory — it is shipped and CFTC-designated.
- Round 1 framed pUSD as potentially explaining withdrawal friction. The primary pUSD doc documents no redemption delay or fee; withdrawal friction on the US venue is a fiat-rail phenomenon (funds-in-flight lockup, FIFO ordering, same-method-return).
